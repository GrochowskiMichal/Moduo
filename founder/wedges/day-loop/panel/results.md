## What would flip a no

- If after the trial I'm still opening it daily without forcing myself and it pulls in both my work Google and personal iCloud calendars, I'd pay.
- Proof of real two-way sync with a work Google or Outlook calendar, plus a few independent reviews or comparison videos from people who switched from Sunsama or Motion and stuck with it for months.
- Seeing a keyboard-first demo with two-way Google Calendar sync and integrations with the tools my company already uses, where the end-of-block reflow takes one shortcut instead of clicks.
- If it worked right on top of my existing Google Calendar, and the free plan let me do the end-of-block push/reflow thing for real, then I'd use it free for a couple of months and maybe pay at around $5 a month.
- If I'm still opening it daily after the trial and those end-of-block prompts can be ignored or batched without anything piling up, I'd pay the $12.
- If it worked as a thin layer on top of my actual Google Calendar, with blocks written straight to GCal and the end-of-block prompt arriving where I already am, I'd try the free plan and maybe pay after the trial.
- Proof of real two-way sync with both Google and Outlook work calendars, plus a full keyboard-driven flow, shown during the no-card trial.
- If the free plan let me run the whole drag-to-calendar and roll-forward loop for a month, and I was still using it at the end, I'd think about paying.
- If I could turn the end-of-block check-in into one quiet end-of-day review, and both my Google and iCloud calendars showed up in one view, and I was still opening it on day 14 of the trial.
- If the free plan ran out of what I need right when the habit stuck, or there was a cheaper planner-only tier around $5-6, I'd pay.
- If the free plan held up for a month or two and I was still opening it every morning without forcing myself, I'd pay. Basically, proof that I don't churn this time.
- If it worked as a thin layer on top of Google Calendar, with blocks written straight into my existing calendar and no switching apps, I might try the free plan for the end-of-block done/push/drop prompt.
